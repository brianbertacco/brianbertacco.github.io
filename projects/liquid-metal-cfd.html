01 / THE QUESTION

What the project explored

The project modelled lead–bismuth eutectic in a single-rod heater and a counter-current straight-tube heat exchanger. The central question was how turbulence and thermal-diffusion assumptions influence the agreement between CFD and experimental temperature profiles.

02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

From problem to method

  • Co-authored the group study with Antonio Morrone and Giulio Riva.
  • The report documents 2D axisymmetric steady-state models, temperature-dependent material properties implemented through Fluent UDFs, and mesh-sensitivity studies.
  • The analysis compares k–ε and k–ω SST models alongside constant and local turbulent-Prandtl-number treatments.
  • The heat-exchanger study includes coupled fluid–solid–fluid heat transfer and an analytical ε–NTU heat balance.

03 / THE OUTCOME

What the work showed

The report finds that a local Kays turbulent-Prandtl-number treatment improved temperature-profile agreement relative to the globally imposed alternative. Remaining discrepancies underline the importance of heat-loss assumptions and the limits of standard RANS closures for liquid metals.

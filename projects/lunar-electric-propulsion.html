01 / THE QUESTION

Project objectives

The study sized an electric-propulsion system for a 400 kg spacecraft travelling from geostationary transfer orbit to a lunar polar orbit. Propellant choice, thruster capability, tank mass and power generation were considered together.

02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

Methods and contribution

  • Co-authored the mission study with Felice Venuti, Aidan Tanner and Alens Snepsts.
  • The team compared xenon, krypton, hydrogen and argon, using system-specific impulse as a selection criterion.
  • Candidate thrusters were checked against power and specific-impulse limits, with the power-generation mass included in the feasibility assessment.

03 / THE OUTCOME

Results

Within the report’s assumptions, xenon and the NSTAR thruster gave the preferred configuration. The selected case reports approximately 43.24 kg of propellant and 73.6 mN average thrust for the specified transfer.

04 / SCOPE & LIMITATIONS

This is a preliminary sizing and selection exercise. It does not establish a fully optimised low-thrust trajectory or flight-qualified propulsion system; the reported comparison depends on the assumed mission and component data.

01 / THE QUESTION

Project objectives

My MSc thesis investigated how material and thickness influence shock transmission at solid–water interfaces. The work combined pulsed-power experiments, high-speed optical diagnostics and pressure measurements with a dedicated data-analysis workflow.

02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

Methods and contribution

  • Conducted experiments using electrically exploding copper foils in water, with PLA, aluminium and copper samples.
  • Tracked transmitted and reflected fronts from high-speed shadowgraph images and analysed hydrophone measurements.
  • Developed the processing workflow for ROI alignment, camera-tilt correction, stack-offset correction and local shock-velocity estimation.
  • Compared acoustic, dynamic and nonlinear Hugoniot approaches to interface transmission.

03 / THE OUTCOME

Results

The measured waves weakened with increasing sample thickness. Copper produced strongly attenuated transmitted waves, while aluminium exhibited a clear dependence on thickness. The comparison also showed why simple acoustic approximations become insufficient for stronger shocks.

04 / SCOPE & LIMITATIONS

The conclusions apply to the tested materials, thicknesses and experimental configuration. Source coupling, material porosity and uncertainty in shock-front tracking affect the interpretation; the study is not a qualification test of a finished protective structure.

01 / THE QUESTION

Project objectives

A cube-shaped satellite model was used to study thermal control under beginning- and end-of-life optical properties and seasonal orbital extremes. Analytical radiator and heater sizing was followed by numerical evaluation in SYSTEMA/THERMICA.

02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

Methods and contribution

  • Contributed to the laboratory work, Part 2 of the report, the Lab D presentation and peer review, as documented in the contribution table.
  • The numerical work compared equipment temperatures with and without internal radiative exchange.
  • Heater powers were adjusted iteratively to reach target duty cycles in nominal cold and survival modes.

03 / THE OUTCOME

Results

The final nominal cold-case duty cycles were 61.9%, 67.5% and 68.5% for the three active heaters. Internal radiation reduced the peak temperature of the hottest node by approximately 9 °C. Survival-mode tuning brought the active heaters to 61%, 67% and 62%.

04 / SCOPE & LIMITATIONS

The model assumes a simplified geometry and prescribed thermal couplings. Some cold-case nodes reached the −10 °C lower limit, leaving tight temperature margins. These are numerical design results, separate from the MIST satellite project.

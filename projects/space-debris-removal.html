01 / THE QUESTION

What the project explored

This five-author group paper evaluates a spacecraft that would apply expanding foam to selected debris objects, increasing their area-to-mass ratio so atmospheric drag accelerates orbital decay. Target choice, rendezvous, propulsion and mission cost were explored at conceptual level.

02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

From problem to method

  • The report credits me with paper writing and debris, spacecraft and cost analysis.
  • The team examined a list of ten target objects and estimated the orbit-transfer needs for visiting them.
  • The spacecraft concept includes Hall-effect electric propulsion and an expanding-foam delivery system.

03 / THE OUTCOME

What the work showed

The study proposes a 2,500 kg servicing spacecraft with four PPS-5000 thrusters, aiming to treat ten objects within a one-year mission. The intended subsequent deorbiting timeframe is 25 years in the study scenario.

04 / SCOPE & LIMITATIONS

This is a preliminary feasibility study with simplified, cooperative debris and idealised foam behaviour. No rendezvous, capture or foam deployment was built or flight tested. Launch and environmental statements in the 2024 course report reflect its scenario assumptions.
